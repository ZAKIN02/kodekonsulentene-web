# Applications: Turning a BrandScript into Marketing Assets

Every asset below is generated FROM the BrandScript (or, for a direct request, a mini-BrandScript of the needed elements). Reuse and expand the script's approved messaging — you may recombine, elaborate, and add supporting copy (advice, tips, examples) that serves it — but never introduce new strategic claims, invented facts, or positioning the script doesn't support. Create, edit, repeat.

## The One-Liner

The most-used asset. Structure: **Character + Problem + Plan + Success** in 1-2 sentences.

Pattern: "Most [customers] struggle with [problem]. [Brand] [plan/mechanism] so they can [success]."

Example: "Most small business owners can't explain what they do, so customers ignore them. StoryBrand gives them a 7-part framework to clarify their message so their business grows."

Tests: Can the ideal customer hear it and think "That's me!"? Can a stranger repeat it after one hearing? Does it spark imagination and intrigue? Every employee should memorize it; it replaces "what do you do?" small talk answers.

## The Controlling Idea

One sentence stating the moral of the brand's story. Derive it by asking: when all subplots are stripped away, what is this brand's story about? It should encode the villain, the customer, and the transformation. Use it as the consistency test for every other asset: if a headline, post, or email does not reinforce the controlling idea, cut or rewrite it.

## Website Wireframe (above the fold first)

Order of sections, each pulling directly from the BrandScript:

1. **Header (above the fold):** A headline stating what you offer in terms of customer survival/success (Element 7 or Element 1 desire). A subhead naming the problem or plan. The direct CTA as the obvious big button, top right and center. An image of a happy customer (success state, not the product alone). The header must pass the "grunt test": a caveman could glance at it and grunt what you offer, how it makes life better, and how to buy.
2. **Stakes section:** short copy on what's at stake (Element 6). One or two lines, not a wall.
3. **Value proposition / success:** 3 results customers experience (Element 7).
4. **Guide section:** empathy line + competency proof (testimonials, logos, stats).
5. **Plan section:** the 3 steps with the plan name.
6. **Transitional CTA section:** the free offer with email capture.
7. **Repeated direct CTA** before the footer.

Rules: cut 50% of the text you think you need. Direct CTA repeated on every scroll-depth. One idea per section. Images show transformed customers, not buildings or handshakes.

## Email Campaigns

Two types, both fed by the BrandScript:

- **Nurture sequence (weekly-ish):** valuable free advice tied to the customer's problem, with a soft P.S. mention of the offer. Pattern per email: talk about the problem, give a tip that solves part of it, position the product as the full solution. It's fine if not everyone opens; you are staying top of mind. Let unsubscribers go; they are not your ideal client.
- **Sales sequence (after a transitional CTA opt-in):** 5-6 emails: (1) deliver the asset, (2) problem + solution, (3) testimonial/case story, (4) overcome a specific objection (use the agreement plan), (5) paradigm shift or scarcity, (6) direct ask with the direct CTA.

Anchor subject lines in the script's sound bites; bodies may expand on them with genuinely useful advice tied to the customer's problem, as long as every email reinforces the controlling idea and adds no new strategic claims. Short, clear, one CTA per email.

## Elevator Pitch / Sales Script

Spoken version of the one-liner expanded with the plan: Problem → empathy → competency (one proof) → plan (3 steps) → direct CTA → success vision. Under 60 seconds. End by asking for the action.

## Tagline Candidates

Compress the controlling idea to 3-6 words focused on the customer result. Test each against: does it state a result the customer experiences? Avoid cleverness that requires decoding.

## Keynote / Presentation Skeleton

1. Open with the customer's problem (all three levels, told as a story about one customer).
2. Introduce the villain.
3. Empathy + competency (briefly).
4. Present the plan as the paradigm shift.
5. Stakes (a pinch).
6. Cast the success vision.
7. Direct call to action.

## Social / Content Angles

Rotate sound bites across posts: problem posts (agitate the villain), empathy posts, customer transformation stories (the strongest content: collect and tell stories of transformation), plan explainers, and success visions. Every post reinforces the controlling idea; anything off script gets cut.
