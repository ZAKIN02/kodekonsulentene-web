# StoryBrand Audit and Editing Checklists

Use for Mode C (audit) and as final QA on any BrandScript or asset. When auditing, score each section, cite the offending copy verbatim, and show a rewritten version.

## Quick Assessment (open every audit with these three)

Rate 1-10:
1. Does the message help customers understand how the brand helps them survive and thrive?
2. Could a customer name the main problem the company solves?
3. Is the message clear and simple?

## BrandScript Audit

**Character**
- Is ONE shared customer desire defined, and is it reflected in the materials (text, images, testimonials)?
- Does the slogan/tagline reflect a RESULT the customer experiences?
- Has all brand-as-hero language been removed?

**Problem**
- Is the customer's internal problem (feeling) present in the materials, not just the external one?
- Is there a single, relatable villain?
- Is there inside language a customer wouldn't use? Flag and translate it.
- Do testimonials talk about problems the brand resolved (not generic praise)?

**Guide**
- Is empathy expressed before competency?
- Are competency signals present (stats, testimonials, logos) but not so heavy the brand becomes the hero?
- Does the material convey "we've been where you are and made it out"?

**Plan**
- Is there a named 3-step process plan? Is each step a customer action?
- Is there an agreement plan countering specific fears?
- Is there a "same but different" positioning statement that instantly communicates what they do?
- Is there a loss leader / lead magnet (free PDF for an email)?

**Call to Action**
- Is there a direct CTA above the fold and on every page?
- Is "the obvious button" the direct CTA, visually distinct?
- Is there a transitional CTA offering real free value, hooked to follow-up email?
- Is the CTA worded as a clear imperative, repeated identically everywhere?

**Failure**
- Is at least one concrete consequence of inaction stated?
- Is negative imagery/text overused? (Salt, not the whole shaker.)

**Success**
- Does the copy state how the customer will FEEL after the internal problem is solved?
- Are results listed (not just features and benefits)?
- Could a salesperson lift a line verbatim to describe the after-state?

**General edits**
- Anything that doesn't fit one of the seven elements: can it be deleted?
- Is each element short and concise? Cut words again.
- Does the tagline reflect a customer result?

## Website Audit (additional)

- Direct CTA above the fold? On every page?
- Passes the grunt test: within 5 seconds, what do you offer, how does it make life better, how do I buy?
- One clear idea communicated, with a result?
- Transitional CTA with email capture?
- Can every word, button, and image be mapped to one of the seven elements? Delete what can't.
- Images of customers experiencing the success state?
- Too much text? Challenge: cut 50%.
- Too many links/menu items? Trim.
- Multiple revenue streams: is there one umbrella message unifying them so customers can "choose their own adventure" without getting lost?

## Email Audit

- Capturing emails in exchange for free value?
- Nurture emails: valuable advice with a soft product mention?
- Sales emails: short, clear, one CTA, deadline where honest?

## Keynote / Pitch Audit

- Opens with the customer's problem, not the company's history?
- Empathy expressed; competency demonstrated without bragging?
- Plan presented as the paradigm shift?
- Stakes named? Success vision cast? Direct ask made?
- Could the whole talk be told as a story about one customer?

## Internal / Staff Audit

- Does the team know the BrandScript? Can each person say the one-liner?
- Is there a line for "same but different" positioning?
- Does staff know when to ask for the purchase and introduce transitional CTAs?
- Is "off script" used as shared vocabulary to flag drifting messaging?

## Audit Output Format

1. Scorecard: the three quick-assessment ratings plus a per-element pass/needs-work table.
2. Top findings: the 3-5 highest-impact problems, each with verbatim quote → rewritten version.
3. Missing elements: which of the seven are absent entirely.
4. Quick wins: changes shippable today (usually headline, CTA button, stakes line).
5. Offer to produce the full corrected BrandScript or rewritten copy.

## Corrected Deliverables

If the user provides the source file (HTML, deck, doc, email), offer a corrected version, not just findings — but create the file only after the user accepts the offer. Preserve their existing design system, structure conventions, and code; change copy and section order only. Annotate major sections with brief comments mapping them to framework elements so the user can see the script at work. Ask where to save it; absent a preference, write it next to the source file with a `-storybrand` suffix (e.g. `homepage-storybrand.html`), never overwriting the original.
